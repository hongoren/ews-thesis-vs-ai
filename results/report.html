Step 1: the thesis model reproduces exactly

Re-estimating Model 1 on the original panel (202,615 firm-quarters, 1,307 defaults) gives the same 39 hazard ratios and p-values as Table 8, with log likelihood −10,636.664 and LR χ² 2,673.37. The out-of-time flag counts also match the thesis: 1,625 firms flagged at the end of 2011 (16.79%) and 1,624 at the end of 2010 (18.53%).

The thesis also tested 2013, but the panel ends in 2012, so all comparisons here predict 2010, 2011 and 2012. Every model is trained only on data available before the year it predicts.

Step 2: how well each model ranks risk

AUC measures how well a model ranks defaulters above non-defaulters (0.5 is random, 1.0 is perfect). These are pooled over the three test years.

Using your thesis measures: sort firms by risk score, flag the top share (selectivity ratio), and see what share of next year's defaulters were flagged (success rate for defaulters). Test year 2012, 9,678 active firms, 156 defaults.

The AI model is not better every time. Trained through 2009 and tested on 2010, right after the crisis, the Cox model ranks slightly better for 1-year prediction (0.726 vs 0.708), though AI still catches more defaulters at the thesis flag rate. With only 25 to 55 defaults per quarter, single-quarter results are noisy; the pooled numbers are the fair summary.

What drives the AI model

Drop in AUC when each variable group is randomly shuffled, 2012 test year. Bigger drop means the model relies on it more.

  • The thesis idea holds up. Core banking behavior data alone (no financial statements) supports a strong early warning model. AI extracts more from the same data.
  • Trends help. Adding 1- and 4-quarter changes and rolling averages of usage, balances and transactions raised 1-year AUC from 0.762 to 0.781 pooled.
  • Reasons can be given per firm. For the highest-risk firms that defaulted in 2012, the top drivers were a fully cash-based exposure, borrowing from many banks at once and high limit usage at other banks.

Method

  • Data: 202,615 firm-quarters, 15,593 borrowers, 1,307 defaults, 2005 Q1 to 2012 Q4. Customer IDs are transformed and no names are included.
  • Thesis Cox: Model 1 with its 39 variables, Breslow ties, analysis time in quarters since credit was granted. Firms are flagged when the risk score is at least 1, as in the thesis.
  • AI: histogram gradient boosting (scikit-learn), target = default within the next 4 quarters (or next quarter), trained only on firm-quarters whose outcome was known before the test year. Fixed settings, no tuning on test years.
  • Tests: scored at the end of 2009, 2010 and 2011 on active, non-defaulted firms; AUC intervals from 500 bootstrap resamples.
Data: core banking panel from a Turkish participation bank, as used in Öngören (2016), An Empirical Study on Credit Early Warning Systems, PhD thesis, Kadir Has University. Analysis rebuilt November 2026.
